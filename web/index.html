How the AI decides

Connect-4 has about 4.5 trillion positions, so the AI cannot look at all of them. It looks a few moves ahead, scores the boards it reaches, and skips branches that cannot change its choice.

1. Minimax

For each column, the AI imagines your best reply, then its best answer, and so on. It assumes you always pick the move worst for it (MIN) and chooses the column whose worst case is best (MAX).

2. Alpha-Beta pruning

Alpha is the score the AI is already sure of; beta is the score you are already sure of. When alpha ≥ beta, that branch can never happen in real play, so it is skipped. The chosen move does not change, only the work.

3. Heuristic score

At the search limit, the board is scored by checking all 69 lines of four: points for the AI's open threes and twos and its centre discs, a penalty for your open threes.

Iterative deepening (Expert level)

Instead of a fixed depth, the AI searches depth 1, then 2, then 3, until its time budget runs out, and keeps the deepest search it finished. Each finished depth's best move is tried first at the next depth, so pruning cuts even more.
